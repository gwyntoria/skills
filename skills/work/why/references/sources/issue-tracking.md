# Issue / Task Tracking

## Evidence to seek

Requirements, business constraints, acceptance criteria, scope changes, and discussion explaining the problem behind a change. Read available issue records through the project's tools or supplied exports; no particular tracker or MCP is required.

## Investigation

1. Start with task IDs linked from commits and PRs. Read descriptions and comments, including scope changes.
2. Search accessible records by feature name, key symbols, error strings, and business terms. Try related wording when exact identifiers return nothing.
3. Follow parent tasks, duplicate chains, milestones, and project context. Distinguish the broader initiative from an individual implementation task.
4. Compare task dates and scope with the code's introduction. For defensive logic, look for incident, reliability, or postmortem follow-up records around that time.
5. Pass linked design documents, PRs, and error events to their owning investigators, retaining the link and why it matters.

## Interpretation limits

- Generic template text and labels suggest a topic; explicit descriptions and comments establish reasons.
- Closed, reopened, or duplicate tasks may reflect different plans. Preserve the relevant chronology.
- Missing permissions or incomplete exports limit coverage; they do not establish that no requirement existed.

## Return

Task ID, title, path or URL; relevant motivation text with its location; author and dates when available; parent, duplicate, and project context; queries performed and coverage limits.
